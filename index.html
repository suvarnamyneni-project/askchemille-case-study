03

Problem

Choosing an engineering polymer is a high-stakes call made early in product design. A wrong grade surfaces late, in validation or tooling, when change is expensive.

How might we help a design engineer turn a plain-language need, or a competitor's grade, into a short, trustworthy list of Celanese grades, and know exactly when to bring in a human expert?

Engineers usually start with a loose need ("heat-resistant, glass-filled, for a fuel cap"), a spec number, or a competitor's grade name, not a Celanese trade name. The catalog is deep: the live search lists 2,115 grades and the resource library 448 documents, each grade carrying dense data on chemistry, filler, processing, chemical resistance and certifications. Keyword search and long filter panels push the translation work onto the user.

Four ways in

Users arrive from alternatives, application, filters, or a live expert, as the site's FAQ lists them. The front door has to honor all four.

Honest AI output

The site labels chemical-resistance data as guidance only, to be validated. A percent match cannot be presented as a specification.

Value before the account

Premium content (documents, quotes, chat) sits behind a free account, so the free path still has to deliver a useful shortlist.

A global, regulated surface

The audience is in two languages, English and 中文, with trademark, GDPR, and cookie-consent requirements that cannot break the flow.

05

End Users

Industries offered as entry points on the site: Automotive and Transportation, Consumer Goods, Electrical and Electronics, Industrial, and Medical and Pharma.

Design engineers at OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers

Need a grade that meets thermal, mechanical and chemical demands for a new part. They often start from an application or a property, not a trade name.

Materials and application engineers

Validate shortlists against specs, OEM approvals, UL listings and chemical-resistance data. They want data sheets and documents they can defend.

Engineers replacing a current material

Start from a competitor grade name and need a like-for-like Celanese alternative, with the variant made explicit before anything is ranked.

Sourcing and procurement specialists

Need quotes, availability and colours once engineering has a shortlist. The path from grade to request-a-quote has to be short.

Sustainability-driven teams

Look for bio-content, carbon-capture or PFAS-alternative grades. That intent is its own search, not a leftover filter.

Opportunity gaps

  • Databases expect users to speak in filters; none of the reviewed pages describe a search that asks the user a clarifying question.
  • Alternatives search is paywalled on UL Prospector and not described elsewhere; a free, explained % match is a clear differentiator.

  • Sustainability and PFAS-alternative discovery is emerging as its own search intent.
  • Most tools gate advanced features by price; a free account gate tied to intent lowers the barrier.

09

Design Decisions

Concept decisions for the guided flows, mapped to the proto-personas. Where the live site already works this way, the audit keeps it. None of this was shipped by this study.

One plain-language search box

Why. Priya does not know trade names and faces a blank page. Example prompts such as "PPS grade for extrusion" show the kind of query that works.

Trade-off. Filter-first people still need "See all grades & filter" beside the field. The search stays the front door; browse stays one click away.

One clarifying question, with Skip

Why. A vague query returns too many grades. The first results screen asks one processing question as chips ("Which processing filter(s)… for 'extrusion'?"), not the full filter set.

Trade-off. Do not force the question. The capture still shows 17 grades before a chip is chosen, so Skip has to remain a real choice rather than a dead end.

Show what the AI understood

Why. Marcus will not trust a black box. Parsed intent becomes removable chips (PPS ×, Profile Extrusion ×) so a wrong reading can be corrected.

Trade-off. More chrome on the results bar, in exchange for a recommendation the user can edit.

Say how many grades, out loud

Why. A conversational count ("We have 17 grades…") and a live "View grades • 2115" in the filter modal stop dead ends and zero-result searches.

Trade-off. A headline count can sound more certain than the match quality. Strong matches and partial matches stay split so the count is not the only signal.

Disambiguate, then rank alternatives

Why. Lin starts from a competitor grade. The assistant asks which Ultramid variant, then ranks Celanese grades by percent match.

Trade-off. The collapsed card shows "98% match to Ultramid® A3WG6 LT BK, +1" without the properties. The concept keeps the orange label and asks it to name the basis. A percent is not a specification.

Strong matches, then partial, then an expert

Why. Marcus fears missing a better grade, and a weak list needs a next step. Strong matches come first, then "Partial matches to consider", then "Need an exact match? Get in touch".

Trade-off. A longer results page. The expert prompt stays so a thin list is not a dead end.

A batch filter for spec-driven search

Why. Marcus needs precise, multi-criteria filtering: nine facet groups, including Sustainable options, PFAS alternatives, Chemical resistance and Specifications.

Trade-off. Explorers stay on one clarifying question. The batch modal, with a live grade count, is reserved for people who already know their criteria.

Action before the long grade page

Why. Marcus and Lin need a quote, documents, or a specialist without scrolling the whole evidence page. Summary, Request quote, View Documents, and jump-to anchors come first.

Trade-off. Chemical resistance, properties, and colours still follow, with the guidance-only caveat kept on chemical resistance.

Keep the shortlist beside the grade

Why. Priya should not bounce between the list and the detail. A strong-match rail stays on the left of the grade page.

Trade-off. A narrower detail column on desktop, so switching grades does not lose the query context.

Gate at the moment of intent

Why. Search and browse stay free. A free account unlocks chat, documents, saved searches, PFAS alternatives and quotes. The gate opens over results the person was already viewing.

Trade-off. Lead capture waits until quote, documents, or another locked action. Arrival is not blocked. The home account card stays a pitch, not a wall.

Chat and email on every screen

Why. Marcus hits edge cases the model should not answer. 24/5 experts stay one click away, and the hand-off can carry the search context.

Trade-off. Always-on contact sits beside self-serve. Both stay, because a rep-free path without an expert is the regret the buying research describes.

Industry as another front door

Why. New users who are not sure how to begin get "Start searching by Industry": Automotive and Transportation, Consumer Goods, Electrical and Electronics, Industrial, Medical and Pharma.

Trade-off. A second entry beside the field. The plain-language search remains the primary door.

Prototype

There is no separate prototype file. The concept is the two flows above, walked against the live desktop screens from 30 Oct 2026. It was not tested with participants.

  1. Home: describe the need

    Type "PPS grade for extrusion" in the hero search, or pick an industry.

  2. AI parses the query

    PPS appears as a removable chip; the headline confirms 17 grades.

  3. Answer one clarifying question

    Choose Profile Extrusion from the processing chips, or Skip; the result count updates.

  4. Scan ranked results

    Strong matches first, partial matches below, and an expert prompt at the end.

  5. Open a grade

    Fortron® FX20T1: summary, key features, chemical resistance, properties, colours.

  6. Act

    Request quote or View Documents opens the free-account gate for guests; chat is always available.

  7. Alternate path

    Search a competitor grade, choose the variant, review % matches, compare.

The walkthrough uses the captured guest path: home, the clarifying question before a chip is chosen, strong matches and partial matches, a competitor-grade percent match, grade detail, the full grade page, and the account gate over results. Loading, zero-result, signed-in, and mobile states were not captured.

Final Design

Annotated screens of the live site (desktop, 1440 px wide, captured 30 Oct 2026). They are the reference for the concept, not a claim that this study shipped them.

Select a screen to enlarge it. Arrow keys move between images. Escape closes the viewer. Without JavaScript, the control opens the image file directly.

Home: the front door

  1. Two-tone hero, "Welcome to the Chemille® Digital Assistant, your gateway to endless materials knowledge", in Midnight Violet and Muted Lilac.
  2. Search field with rotating example prompts teaches the kind of query that works.
  3. Primary "See all grades & filter" for filter-first users; tonal "Quick guide" for newcomers.
  4. Right column: 31-video tutorial playlist and a free-account value card with six benefits.
  5. "Start searching by Industry" menu for users unsure where to begin.

Guided results: the clarifying question

  1. Query stays editable at the top, with a save-search action.
  2. Parsed intent becomes a filter chip (PPS ×) beside Filters and Add Filter.
  3. Assistant headline states the count, then asks one question: processing type for "extrusion".
  4. Answer chips (Film, Other, Profile, Sheet Extrusion) plus Skip keep control with the user.
  5. Sort and colour controls sit above cards; an in-list card invites a free account.

Alternatives: % match to a competitor grade

  1. Assistant confirms "11 grades similar to 'ultramid a3wg6'" and asks which variant.
  2. Variant chips disambiguate before ranking, preventing a wrong baseline.
  3. Results sorted by relevance; each card shows the match in orange, e.g. "98% match to Ultramid® A3WG6 LT BK, +1".

Grade detail: evidence and action

  1. Strong-match rail on the left keeps the shortlist one click away.
  2. Grade name, polymer and a one-sentence summary, then Request quote and View Documents.
  3. Jump-to anchors: Chemical Resistance, Properties, Available Grade Colors, Talk to a specialist.
  4. Chemical-resistance tables by chemical family, with a guidance-only disclaimer.
  5. Custom-colour prompt with Email Us and Chat With Us.

Account gate: value at the moment of intent

  1. Opens on gated actions, over the results the user was already viewing.
  2. Title "Sign in or create an account." with "free" in Electric Violet.
  3. Six benefits with green checks and lock icons show exactly what unlocks.
